Help & Support

How it works, what to expect, and what to do when something's off.

How it works

Open a song and it's fed through HT-Demucs, Meta's separation network, which pulls out drums, bass, guitar, keys, vocals and everything else. The vocals then go through a second model, trained for karaoke, which lifts the lead singer off the backing vocals. Everything runs on your own computer; no audio ever leaves it.

What to expect

The first run downloads the models (about 190 MB in total). Separating takes a few minutes per song on the processor — a progress bar shows where it's at, and the window stays usable. A song you've split before opens instantly from the library.

Getting just one part, or everything but one

Every stem has a switch. Turn off the ones you don't want and export as one mix to get, say, an instrumental with no lead vocal. Solo a stem and export to get it alone. Or export the selected stems as separate files for your DAW.

When the split isn't clean

Separation is a best guess, not a master recording. Heavily layered vocals, distorted guitars and dense synths can bleed between stems — that's the state of the art, not a fault. Pads, organs and synths often land in Aux rather than Keys.
